Specification Register Search

Working prototype

Finds the closest existing specification for a new enquiry in minutes, by scoring every entry in the register against the enquiry.

The challenge. Before quoting, an existing approved specification that matches the client's request has to be found. These sat across thousands of technical documents and reports, and finding one took about two hours of opening files one by one.

What I built. I restructured the specification register first, then had AI tools build a search on top of it. Type an enquiry in plain words; the tool ranks every entry and shows exactly which values match.

  • Plain-language search: type the enquiry in ordinary words and the specification fields fill themselves
  • Weighted match score across the key specifications, counting only what was asked
  • Partial credit for near values; missing data shown as "Not recorded", never hidden
  • One tool for two departments, each with its own filters
  • Reads newer register files on the user's device only; nothing uploaded
  • Every match is confirmed by the specialist team before quoting
  1. BottleneckHours per search
  2. Register rulesTabs, links, cut-off date
  3. FiltersFrom two user teams
  4. AI buildSearch and scoring
  5. TestReal enquiries, v2

My decisionsShared or AI-built

Result
A search that took about two hours now takes about three minutes. The client asked for a full build, and a second department asked to be added.
Also fits
Product catalogues, equipment specifications, tender libraries, standard contracts. The demo shows the same logic on a footwear catalogue.

3D Product Visualiser and Document Cross-Check

In use

Interactive 3D models of technical products built from their drawings, with a parts list and a cross-check of the documents.

The challenge. I sell technical products and their components. Flat drawings and technical reports are hard for a commercial person to read, and the documents for one product are rarely checked against each other.

What I built. Rotatable 3D models of many products, first on an earlier AI tools account, each linked to a parts list where every value shows its source.

  • Preset views, measurements, and colour coding by design data
  • Tap a part to highlight it; quantities and specifications listed by section
  • Each value tagged: from the drawings, calculated, or inferred and to confirm
  • Masses recalculated from geometry and compared with the technical report
  • Labelled as a visual aid only, never for fabrication

Quote Filing Reconciliation

Completed its purpose

Compares the quotation register with the filing folder, lists every quote with no PDF, and tracks the backlog until it is clear.

The challenge. After a portfolio handover, nobody knew how far filing had fallen behind, and checking over a hundred quotes against a folder by hand is slow and error-prone.

What I built. A reconciliation that turns the gap into a working checklist: what is missing, what can be skipped, and what needs a second look.

  • Register matched against folder contents on a stated date
  • Cancelled and replaced quotes grouped apart, so no time is wasted on them
  • Revised quotes and entries with no status flagged for checking
  • Search, filters and a progress bar while working through the list
Result
The filing backlog has been cleared.
Also fits

AI Inbox Action Briefing

Workflow in daily personal use

On request, AI tools reads the inbox and sent items and builds a dated action list, coloured by whose move it is.

The challenge. A sales role runs on email, and important actions get buried. It is hard to see at a glance what is waiting on me and what is waiting on someone else.

What I built. A repeatable briefing, refined over 17 versions, with categories I defined: Urgent, To do, Waiting, Done and New since last update.

  • Sent items count as replies, so answered threads drop off
  • I add what happened offline: calls and meetings
  • Grouped by when it is due, then waiting on others, then closed
  • Next step: merging into the Key Account CRM
Result
A faster start to the day and fewer dropped follow-ups.

Batch PDF Stamp & e-Signature

Working

Stamps and signs a whole batch of PDF documents in one go, entirely in the browser.

The challenge. Released documents each need the same approval stamp, details, date and signature. Doing it file by file in a PDF editor is slow, and the position and size are never quite the same twice.

What I built. One page: fill in the stamp once, draw or upload a signature, drop in any number of PDFs and download them all stamped and signed as a single ZIP. Nothing leaves the computer.

  • Signature drawn on a pad or uploaded, remembered for next time
  • Stamp on the first, every or last page; size set automatically by page size or chosen
  • White backing so the stamp stays readable over busy drawings
  • Many files at once, returned as one ZIP
  • All processing on the user's own device, so there is no upload and no licence
  1. BottleneckStamping file by file
  2. RulesContent and placement
  3. AI buildpdf-lib, JSZip
  4. TestMixed page sizes
  5. RefineAuto size, backing
Result
One step instead of file by file, with an identical stamp on every page.
Also fits
Approved stamps on supplier documents, receiving stamps on delivery notes, controlled-copy marking, audit evidence packs.

3D Product Configurator and Assembly Guide

Working

Shows how a family of mounting components fits together and adjusts, checks the load, and lists the inconsistencies it finds in the drawings.

The challenge. Sales staff need to explain how configurable components fit, adjust and differ between variants, and the drawings for one product family do not always agree with each other.

What I built. An interactive 3D guide built from the production and assembly drawings, with a cross-check of the sheets that I asked for.

  • Choose a family and series; assembled, exploded or step-by-step build
  • Adjustment settings shown against the angle of the mounting surface
  • Loading check against the limits printed on the drawings
  • Moving-part demonstration and a variant comparison table
  • Drawing inconsistencies graded: could affect ordering, or housekeeping
Result
Product training without a workshop visit, and drawing conflicts raised before anything is ordered.

Interactive Event Platform

Built and tested

Beyond business: web apps for hosted murder-mystery evenings, run under my own events brand (shown here as Example Mystery Evenings).

The challenge. An interactive evening for 15 guests means private character briefs, clues released at the right moment, and votes counted fast at the finale. Paper packs get lost or read by the wrong person.

What I built. I wrote the storylines and designed the game flow; AI tools built the apps. Built and tested with groups of people, ahead of a public launch.

  • Secret dossiers encrypted (AES-GCM), each opened only with the guest's own code
  • Evidence released in stages with codes the host announces
  • Guests vote from their own phones; only the host sees the live tally
  • Paper-slip entry and WhatsApp or email backup if the connection fails
  • Host guides, story bibles and a printable cast sheet for each storyline
Why it matters
Secure design, real-time multi-user apps and guest-facing polish under pressure. These are the same skills my business tools rely on.
Also fits
Training simulations, personalised secure briefings, live polling at supplier days and conferences.
